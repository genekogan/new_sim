## Minimum contract

`paired_walk { actors:[a,b], rendezvous:[[x,z],[x,z]], destination:[[x,z],[x,z]], speed:0.9 }` binds the pair and two parallel ground lanes once. Each main lane is 3–12 m with equal displacement; lateral spacing is 0.8–1.2 m. Default speed is 0.9 m/s, bounded to 0.3–1.0. A wide clear straight corridor and no other person in that corridor are required. Regroup routes are ground-only, at most30 m (`PAIR_MAX_REGROUP_DISTANCE`; root-authorized new scope after checking actual departure distances). Initial free hands, ground readiness, foreign ownership, malformed geometry, actual link-dependent regroup/travel routes and infeasible regroup/corridor refuse before child effects. An unrelated usable stair elsewhere in the scene is allowed.

Ordinary `walk_to` children first reach the two regroup spots and align toward the committed destination; both main walks start together at the same speed with `parent` and `preempt:false`. Physical poses, collision, acceleration, traffic and clearance remain the walker's responsibility. Actual completed children plus actual endpoint proximity certify joint arrival. There is no elapsed-time success, geometry override, active walk retargeting or speed mutation.

`paired_walk_control { walk, intent:pause|resume|end }` operates on that exact action. Pause cancels/drains owned children and retains the destination. An independent directive takes precedence for its actor; the other owned walk drains too and the invitation remains waiting. Child interruption, blocked movement, separation beyond1.8 m or100 ticks without physical progress gives a sticky concrete diagnostic, with no resubmission until explicit Resume. Resume computes a local regroup cross-section from both current positions projected onto the original route; ordinary new children reach it and realign before remaining travel. End is terminal and releases the pair's logical claims after owned cleanup; it cannot cancel a foreign directive.

`readPairedWalk(rt,id)` returns detached bindings, mode, regroup spots, physical separation, per-person route progress/certified arrival, owned action IDs and diagnostic. Successful arrival remains certified if a later independent command moves an actor. Active snapshot restoration explicitly refuses in this first scope. Pair claims use `paired-walk:<actor>`; physical actor claims belong only to ordinary children.
