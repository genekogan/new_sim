## Behavior and authority

`IntentPublisher.interpretAndPublish` captures the original body/Mind/current
principal and exact edited purpose before awaiting interpretation. Its callback
receives that same guard for the real compiler's mint/deal admissions. The guard
is checked after the callback and again under the publication writer lock.
Unrelated purposes and ordinary movement do not invalidate an exact edit.
Mind replacement, current authority loss, a competing edit or a later withdrawal
refuse stale effects. Already accepted earlier effects retain their facts.

`Brain.compile(..., 'intent-v2', guard, journal)` is an explicit backend alignment
mode: ordinary present directions can produce the existing `now` trigger without
requiring the word NOW. The legacy boolean alignment still requires explicit
NOW. One general trigger/agenda mechanism remains; no timetable/routine object.
The prompt preserves optional/time/gathering/negated meaning. Synthetic tests
qualify transport, validation and mode separation, not live interpretation
accuracy for arbitrary phrasing. That comparison needs a later bounded workload.

Only an explicitly constructed Brain with `intentV2Choices:true` uses private
retained purposes. It filters withdrawn/fulfilled and other-owner entries,
weighs strict/importance/context, includes original words and accepted meanings
in that Soul's private mid request, and wakes on a newly published revision.
For this first bridge, a new immediate single-step purpose supplies a temporary
planning view; it does not replace a legacy agenda or older independent purposes.
A new standing purpose cannot reissue an older immediate direction. Existing
legacy NOW retains precedence. Native exit/cancellation/approach controllers
perform every physical transition; no pose jump or new contact permission.

Choice admission rechecks original Mind/body/owner/current private context and
quiet state under the sequencer lock. A withdrawal accepted before the prepared
choice prevents its admission. Current model context changes invalidate delayed
choices; they do not rewrite accepted historical decisions.
