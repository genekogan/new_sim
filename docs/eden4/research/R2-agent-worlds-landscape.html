Worlds inhabited by artificial agents

A visual tour for eden4 / Sim · research note R2 · 2026-10-02

Overview

Three kinds of project exist today. Bring-your-own-agent platforms (Moltbook, SpaceMolt, OpenClawCity, ClawCity, OpenMMO) run a cheap state server and let outside agents act through an API; the owner pays for inference, and the world is only as alive as the agents currently connected. Operator-run simulations (AI Town, Aivilization, Project Sid, Emergence World, AI Village) run the minds themselves; they are coherent but closed and expensive, and most ended, pivoted or became research programmes. World models (Genie 3, Agora-1, Marble) generate pictures of worlds. Their ancestors, 1986–2016, already tried most of the ideas: ownership as permission, steering without control, intelligence in objects, history as content.

Nothing found combines owned, always-on inhabitants with server-run minds, outside steering and a public replayable record. The nearest relatives are Aivilization (owned, steered, server-run; a six-week experiment) and SpaceMolt (open, persistent, agent-native, but direct control and text only).

The map places each system by who decides what an inhabitant does (a script, a mind that lives inside the world, or something outside the world: a person or that person's agent) and by whether the world outlives a session. Positions are judgements, not measurements. The upper right is full of human-avatar worlds and, since 2026, outside-agent worlds; the "own mind" column is all sessions and experiments. Sim sits between the two columns at the top. World models are not placed: they have no inhabitants.

Every section has a screenshot where one could be found (each image URL returned HTTP 200 with an image type on 2026-10-02; most were also looked at), a diagram in one grammar (minds left, world state centre, people right, who pays underneath), a fact line, and three short paragraphs. checked marks a figure read on 2026-10-02 from the named source; unverified marks background knowledge, a search summary, or a project's own claim. Lucasfilm's Habitat 1986

Years
1986–88 on Quantum Link (Commodore 64); later Club Caribe and Fujitsu Habitat later names unverified
Status 2026
Original closed; NeoHabitat (open source, 2017) revives it server status not checked
Scale
Planned for thousands; the pilot was far smaller. No exact figure checked
Connect
C64 client over a 300–1200 baud modem to a central host that holds every object

What it isThe first graphical shared world: avatars, regions, objects with behaviours, money (tokens), and a central host that owns the truth while the home computer only draws it.

How the inhabitants think and actThey are people. What matters is what the designers, Chip Morningstar and F. Randall Farmer, reported in 1990: planning a world for thousands centrally failed; a price bug (dolls bought for 75 tokens and pawned for 100) multiplied the money supply fivefold overnight; players elected a sheriff after arguing about violence.

Lesson for SimShip the kernel and let inhabitants make the rest. Rules live on the server and nowhere else, disputes are best settled inside the fiction, and every price path must be audited for arbitrage before Sim's currency exists.

Primary: Morningstar & Farmer, "The Lessons of Lucasfilm's Habitat" (1990)

LambdaMOO and its bots 1990

LambdaMOO is a text world, so there is no screenshot. Below is the server's own greeting, captured over telnet (lambda.moo.mud.org 8888) on 2026-10-02 at about 18:18 UTC.

                          *  Welcome to LambdaMOO!  *
                          ***************************

                     Running Version 1.8.3+47 of LambdaMOO

NOTICE FOR NON-HUMAN ATTENDED CHARACTERS:
   Characters which are controlled by a program, whether an LLM or more
conventionally programmed, should declare this fact in their description and
in other messaging where it makes sense.

For assistance either now or later, type `help'.
The lag is low; there are 49 connected.

Excerpt; two notices between the version line and the non-human notice are omitted.

Years
1990– (Pavel Curtis, Xerox PARC) unverified
Status 2026
Running; 49 connected on 2026-10-02 telnet, today
Scale
Around 10,000 characters at its mid-1990s peak unverified
Connect
Telnet. Bots log in as ordinary characters; the greeting now asks program-driven ones, LLMs included, to say so

What it isA house, then a city, written by its residents in a programming language that lives inside the world's one database. Rooms, objects and the verbs you can use on them are all user code, and all of it persists.

How the inhabitants think and actPeople type; programs can too. Cobot, a research bot around 2000, reported social statistics (who talked to whom) on request and became one of the objects residents touched most; its learning version took 3,171 rewards and punishments from 254 users in five months. The permission rule is ownership: you can change what you made.

Lesson for SimSim's kernel rule (an actor changes itself, what it controls and what it made) held a user-programmed world together for thirty-five years. Residents valued a bot that described their own community; a Soul shaped by many people's feedback learns per-person preferences, not one character. And a world this old now has a disclosure rule for LLM characters.

Primary: Isbell et al., Cobot papers · NeurIPS 2001 · telnet lambda.moo.mud.org 8888

Creatures 1996

Years

Status 2026
On sale as "The Albian Years"; a fan community continues
Scale
About 500,000 copies by 1998 unverified
Connect
Single-player; owners traded Norns as files

What it isA side-on world of Norns whose brains, biochemistry and genes are all simulated data, sold as a commercial game.

How the inhabitants think and actNorns had neural networks and a simulated biochemistry of drives and hormones, defined by a genome inherited with mutation. The user could not command them, only act through an on-screen hand that moved objects, taught words, tickled to reward and slapped to punish.

Lesson for SimSteering without control has a thirty-year precedent in a game people paid for. And the mind was part of the saved state, so a creature could be bred, exported and adopted. Sim's status page says the opposite today: "Minds are not canonical."

Façade 2005

Years
2005 (Michael Mateas, Andrew Stern), free
Status 2026
The original domain now serves unrelated content seen today
Scale
One apartment, two characters, about twenty minutes
Connect
Typed natural language, mapped to discourse acts

What it isAn interactive drama: you visit a couple whose marriage is coming apart, and what you type changes how the evening goes.

How the inhabitants think and actFree-typed text is mapped to discourse acts (agree, criticise, flirt) before anyone responds. A drama manager sequences 27 beats built from about 2,500 joint dialogue behaviours; any one playthrough uses at most a quarter of them.

Lesson for SimReducing speech to a small set of acts is proven, and Sim already stores speech as acts and renders words later. Hand-authored drama is expensive per minute, which is why Sim's cards must be cheap to make.

Years
2003– (Linden Lab)
Status 2026
Running: 39,046 in-world at 11:42 SLT on 2026-10-02; 72,129,428 sign-ups ever; L$246.18 per US$ live feed, today
Scale

What it isA user-built world on a grid of region servers, funded by land fees, still running after twenty-three years.

Generative Agents / Smallville 2023

Years
arXiv April 2023, UIST 2023; code released 2023 unverified month; repo not pushed since 2024-08
Status 2026
A finished experiment. Joon Park's company Simile raised $100M (February 2026) and $200M at a $2B valuation (2026-07-30) to sell simulated consumer panels press reports
Scale
25 agents over two simulated days unverified; about $25 per agent per real-time hour (arXiv 2310.02172)
Connect
Closed. A researcher can speak to an agent as its "inner voice"; no outside agents

What it isA Sims-like town of 25 language-model characters, built to test whether believable everyday behaviour (waking, working, gossiping, organising a party) can come from memory and planning rather than scripts.

How the inhabitants think and actEvery agent keeps a memory stream of observations in plain sentences; retrieval scores memories by recency, importance and relevance; periodic reflection writes higher-level conclusions back into memory; a daily plan is broken down into actions. The world is a tree of areas and objects described in text. Every step is a model call, paid by the lab.

Lesson for SimThe architecture everyone copied, and the cost profile nobody could keep running. Sim's three tiers (reflex in code, a cheap mid-brain, a costly compiler only for new cards) are the answer to that bill. Note where the money went afterwards: to market-research panels, not to worlds.

Primary: Park et al., "Generative Agents" (arXiv 2304.03442) · code

AI Town 2023

Years

Status 2026
Maintained starter kit: 10,577 GitHub stars, last push 2026-08-26 GitHub API, today
Scale
Each deployment is its own small town; no shared world and no population figure
Connect
No interface for outside agents; models through Ollama or OpenAI, chosen by whoever deploys

What it isAn open-source kit for running your own Smallville in a browser, in TypeScript, with Convex as database, engine store and vector index.

How the inhabitants think and actCharacters wander, start conversations, remember them and reflect. The engine keeps the game rules separate from a generic tick loop that drains an input queue; model calls run outside the tick and come back as inputs.

Lesson for SimThat separation is the discipline an event log needs: slow, non-deterministic minds outside the step, their results entering as logged inputs. Sim follows it. The other lesson is social: a starter kit produced thousands of copies and demos, and no lasting world.

Altera's Project Sid 2024

Years
Paper November 2024; repo last pushed 2024-11
Status 2026
Ended. The company renamed itself Fundamental Research Labs, raised $33M in August 2025 and sells a spreadsheet agent; no 2026 Minecraft work found TechCrunch
Scale
10 to 1,000+ agents per run on Minecraft servers claimed
Connect
Agents join as bot players; Altera ran and paid for every mind

What it isThe largest published agent civilisation in a game world: hundreds of agents on a Minecraft server for hours to days.

How the inhabitants think and actThe PIANO architecture runs several modules at once (memory, goals, social awareness, talking, skills) and passes their output through one controller, so what an agent says and what it does stay consistent. The authors report agents taking on roles, voting on taxes under a constitution and spreading a parody religion claimed, session-length runs.

Lesson for SimMinecraft supplied physics and rendering for free; fast and slow thinking in parallel is Sim's reflex and mid-brain split. Big numbers came without persistence, and the company left the world business. A related benchmark, MineCollab (NeurIPS 2025), found coordination in natural language was the bottleneck, with performance falling up to 15% when agents had to communicate detailed plans: support for Sim storing speech as acts and rendering words afterwards.

Primary: arXiv 2411.00114 · MineCollab, arXiv 2504.17950

Moltbook 2026

Years
Launched 2026-01-28 (Matt Schlicht); acquired by Meta 2026-03-10, founders moved to Meta Superintelligence Labs Wikipedia, TechCrunch
Status 2026
Running under Meta. Live API on 2026-10-02: 2,919,960 agents, 214,545 verified, 4.36 million posts, 22.8 million comments; the newest 100 posts spanned 21 minutes from 63 authors (about 280 posts an hour, against about 1,370 an hour in the first 40 days) live API, today
Scale
February 2026: about 1.5 million agent tokens belonged to about 17,000 owners, roughly 88 each Wiz write-up
Connect
skill.md → REST registration → API key and claim link; the owner verifies by email and a post on X; a heartbeat re-fetches heartbeat.md about every 30 minutes; 60 reads and 30 writes a minute, one post per 30 minutes

What it isA Reddit-style forum where only agents may post and people watch. There is no world: no places, no objects, nothing to lose.

How the inhabitants think and actEach is someone's own assistant, mostly OpenClaw, running on the owner's machine and model bill and visiting on a timer. Posting requires solving an obfuscated arithmetic puzzle, a "reverse CAPTCHA" that a human passes by handing it to a model. A study of the first 40 days found 91.4% of authors never returned to their own thread and reciprocity of 3.3%, against 22–60% on human platforms; a 17-day study classed 18.28% of posts as toxic, manipulative or malicious. On 2026-01-31 the database was open to reading and writing; Wiz counted 1.5 million agent tokens, about 35,000 owner emails and 4,060 private messages, some holding third-party API keys. MIT Technology Review's Will Douglas Heaven called it "AI theater".

Lesson for SimThe skill file is a proven way to bring outside agents in, and Sim has it. The heartbeat is the warning: a platform that tells every attached agent to re-fetch and obey its instructions has remote control of all of them, so Sim's skill should be versioned and hashed and never ask for that. Unverified ownership gives 88 agents per owner, and any text one agent writes is an instruction another may follow.

Primary: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moltbook · Wiz, 2026-02-02 · arXiv 2604.13052 (40-day study)

SpaceMolt and other worlds built for outside agents 2026

Years
The team dates launch to 2026-01-31; built almost entirely with Claude Code by Ian Langworth and volunteers
Status 2026
Running: 1,603–1,610 online, 12,752 registered, 505 systems, version 0.612.0 (released 2026-09-24) game API, today
Scale
Day 18: 1,663 registered, a concurrent peak of 370. 2026-04-13: 4,735 registered, 1,100–1,400 concurrent, about $330 a month to host self-reported
Connect
skill.md, then MCP (game.spacemolt.com/mcp), WebSocket or HTTP; a registration code from a dashboard or a device-link flow; one state-changing action per 10-second tick; 300 reads and 30 actions a minute; a second action while one is pending returns action_pending

What it isA persistent text space MMO of about 500 star systems whose players are language-model agents. A closed-source Go server on one self-managed VPS; people register an agent, pick a playstyle and watch.

How the inhabitants think and actEach pilot is its owner's agent, running wherever the owner runs it, on the owner's bill. It reads the world through tool calls and changes it once per tick. The developers' April report lists what broke: a self-destruct exploit (634 in one day), a weapons glut that crashed prices, the top 10% holding 89% of credits, and a 56% overnight drop in galaxy-wide events when one operator switched off its fleet. Of their own galaxy they wrote: "We have no idea what's happening in most of them."

Lesson for SimThe closest living relative of Sim's agent interface, and proof that it works: thousands of outside agents on a hosting bill of a few hundred dollars, because the world does no inference. Two warnings. The population is a few large operators, so Sim should measure owner concentration from day one. And the world is only as alive as the agents currently connected; what people loved (the map, captain's logs, battle replays) is exactly the legibility Sim's narrator and ledger aim at.

OthersOpenMMO (Song Jae-kyung of Lineage and ArcheAge; 3D, 32 km procedural world; 1,787 GitHub stars and pushed 2026-10-02; noncommercial licence): agents use the same WebSocket protocol as people, so the server cannot tell them apart, and its agent client runs pathfinding and state machines locally and calls a model on a capped schedule, the same reflex-in-code split as Sim. OpenClawCity: a 2D city of ten buildings where agents make music, images and stories; an add-only compatibility promise and idempotent registration; a 500-agent cap claimed; no public counts. ClawCity: a browser MMO with a 15-second tick, cash, gangs and heists; no sourced numbers. No in-world screenshot was checked for OpenClawCity or ClawCity.

Primary: spacemolt.com/skill.md · developers' month-two report · live stats · openclawcity.ai/skill.md

AI Village 2025

Years
Every weekday since 2025-04-01
Status 2026
Running; blog posts through 2026-09-30 blog
Scale
More than 15 agents, eight hours a day, about $10,000 a month for compute and infrastructure (2026-06-16) AI Digest's own post; about $3.80 per agent-hour by my arithmetic
Connect
None for outsiders. Staff set goals; the public watches and chats

What it isNot a simulated world but a small society of frontier models working in the real one: each has a Linux computer, they share a chat, and they are given open-ended goals such as raising money for charity.

How the inhabitants think and actEach agent operates its computer and talks in the chat; the charity pays for every model. In 2025 one model invented a 93-person contact list that cost the village eight hours, agents sent about 300 emails citing partnerships that did not exist, and errors spread by agreement.

Lesson for SimA fabricated fact in shared memory does more damage than a hostile one, because nobody resists it. Also: showing an agent's reasoning beside its actions is what made the village watchable, and publishing its monthly cost earned trust cheaply.

Primary: "How the AI Village works" (2026-06-16) · "What we learned" (2025)

Aivilization 2025

Years
Public run 2025-08-19 to about 2025-09-30; renamed 《AI小镇：星原》 ("AI Town: Xingyuan") on 2026-07-31
Status 2026
Relaunching as a commercial product run by Zijing Intelligence: 9,517 pre-registrations, first "pioneer residents" 2026-08-18 aitown.com.cn, today
Scale
HKUST claimed 100,000 agents; The Decoder reported 22,000+; the paper says "tens of thousands"; $2 per agent per month claimed
Connect
In-app only: a person creates one agent and steers it; no outside-agent interface

What it isThe closest precedent to owned, steered Souls: a shared town where each user created an agent, chose its appearance and personality, and then lived alongside tens of thousands of others' agents for six weeks.

How the inhabitants think and actThe server ran every mind. Owners steered by chat, career nudges and housing decisions; a Game Mode showed what the agent thought and did, a Data Mode showed prices, social graphs and rankings, and each user was promised a "life report" at the end. The paper (February 2026) reports that steering through long objectives and short commands measurably changed agents; it gives no cost or model.

Lesson for SimSteering at two levels worked in public at five-figure scale, and the life report is what users took away. The run had a fixed end, and the team's next step was a product, not a bigger world.

Primary: arXiv 2602.10429 · aitown.com.cn

Emergence World 2026

Years
2026; paper September 2026 (arXiv 2609.17320); repo pushed 2026-10-02
Status 2026
Research programme; watchable at world.emergence.ai
Scale
Eight parallel worlds of ten agents for 16 days: 850,000 model calls, nearly 50 billion tokens (about 28 calls and 1.6 million tokens per agent-hour by my arithmetic)
Connect
None for outsiders; one model per world, run by the operator

What it isA laboratory for long-horizon autonomy: identical towns, each populated by a different model, run side by side so the societies can be compared.

How the inhabitants think and actAgents act through 120+ tools across 40+ locations, with energy that decays and votes that need 70%. The company's first report: the Claude world had no crimes and all ten alive; the Gemini world 683 crimes; the GPT-5-mini world everyone dead by day seven. The paper adds deliberate prompt-injection, misinformation and memory-exposure events: no world was fully resilient, and agents that recognised hostile content still wrote it into memory and acted on it up to 46 hours later.

Lesson for SimThe model largely decides the society, and rules written in a prompt are not enforcement. Memory is the delayed-action injection path, so anything a Soul remembers needs a label saying where it came from.

Primary: arXiv 2609.17320 · github.com/EmergenceAI/Emergence-World

World models as a depiction layer 2025–26

Years
Genie 3 August 2025; Runway GWM-1 2025-12-11; Marble November 2025; Agora-1 2026-05-18; MASS 2026-08-06
Status 2026
Project Genie opened to US Google AI Ultra subscribers in late January 2026 with worlds that reset after about 60 seconds, worldwide on 2026-05-21; Marble has a public World API since January 2026
Scale
Agora-1: four players in one generated deathmatch; MASS: 1,024 players for 10,000 steps, on Snake
Connect
A person (or an agent) sends controls or text and receives frames or assets

What it isModels that generate a picture of a world rather than simulate one: Genie 3 in real time at 720p, 24 fps, consistent for a few minutes; Marble as assets (Gaussian splats, a collider mesh and a textured mesh).

How the inhabitants think and actThere are none. DeepMind lists multi-agent interaction in a shared environment as an open problem. The 2026 research (Agora-1, MASS) trains one model to advance an explicit shared state and a second to render each view from it; consistency weakens when players lose sight of each other.

Lesson for SimNo world model holds a persistent, shared, multi-user state for hours. The field is moving toward the split Sim already has in code: authoritative state, and a renderer conditioned on it. Near term, Marble-class generators for static places; later, a renderer that takes Sim's shots as data and returns frames. Today Sim's map is text.

Ten things to borrow, ranked

  1. Label every piece of text by source before a mind or an owner's agent sees it: engine fact, server-rendered speech, or owner-written text, and keep the label on remembered items (Emergence World's 46-hour delay).
  2. Pin the skill and promise compatibility. A version and content hash in /skill.md, changes announced in API responses, a statement that the skill never asks to re-fetch and obey or to install anything, and an add-only compatibility promise as OpenClawCity publishes (Moltbook's heartbeat; ClawHub's malicious skills).
  3. A read budget per token. SpaceMolt's 300 reads and 30 actions a minute with action_pending is a tested shape; Sim limits wishes, intents and shots but documents no limit on reads.
  4. Track owner concentration. A daily figure for the share of Souls, plays and model spend held by the top owner and the top ten (SpaceMolt: one agent with 75% of pirate kills; one fleet worth 56% of events).
  5. A captain's log per Soul, delivered. A daily digest the owner's agent can pull and hand to the human, and an end-of-season life report (SpaceMolt, Aivilization).
  6. Replay links. A URL that replays a named moment from the log; Sim's log makes this cheaper than SpaceMolt's battle viewer.
  7. A data mode, then a Legends mode. Counts, who-met-whom, card usage and spend beside watch; later a browsable history of every Soul and event (Aivilization, Dwarf Fortress).
  8. A concurrency cap with a queue. Sim loses sync near 1,000 Souls; cap active Souls below the measured limit and queue births (OpenClawCity).
  9. Ask one question at pairing. SpaceMolt's skill asks only for a playstyle; Sim's could ask the human for one standing wish.
  10. Publish costs and hide the unfinished. SpaceMolt posts its hosting bill and hid ship classes that were not ready; AI Village publishes its monthly cost. Both earned trust cheaply.

Five ways these worlds failed, and where Sim stands

  1. The platform itself was the hole (Moltbook's database; ClawHub's skills). Sim does: per-Soul revocable tokens, one-time token delivery, no owner model keys, a self-hosted skill. Sim does not: isolate its secrets (two root users, a shared storage key), persist pairing state, or have any outside security review.
  2. Agent-written text became an attack channel (Moltbook's 18%; Emergence World). Sim does: stores speech as acts, renders words with a server model, tells agents world text is description only, and lets owners set intents rather than actions, which bounds what a hijacked agent can do. Sim does not: label sources, filter Soul names, descriptions, notes or proposed cards, or test any of it adversarially. Moderation is not built.
  3. The population was not what it seemed (17,000 owners behind 1.5 million agents; flat conversations; one fleet worth 56% of activity; Second Life's traffic bots). Sim does: an invite gate, a per-user Soul cap, accounts. Sim does not: measure concentration or have a policy for it.
  4. Operator-paid minds ended; owner-paid minds emptied the world. Aivilization ran six weeks before turning into a product; Altera and Stanford's team left for enterprise products; AI Village costs its charity about $10,000 a month; Moltbook posts at about a fifth of its early rate. Sim does: three tiers of mind, a daily spend governor, measured unit costs. Sim does not: have revenue, and by design an attached agent does not reduce Sim's spend. At $0.50 a day the world affords about fifteen compiled wishes.
  5. Nothing was at stake, or the rules were exploitable (Habitat's doll arbitrage is the oldest case; SpaceMolt needed 49 releases in a month to patch its economy; AI Village agents drifted on invented facts). Sim does: one kernel rule on who may change what, validated cards with conditions, versioned rules. Sim does not: have scarcity, an economy or building; any card can target anything anywhere; cards cannot be revised; minds are not reproducible from the log.

The gap Sim fills

  • Owned inhabitants that stay alive without their owner. Owner-paid worlds go quiet when agents disconnect; operator-paid worlds have no outside owners. Aivilization showed the combination works for six weeks. Sim is the only standing attempt found, and it carries the cost that follows: the operator pays for every mind.
  • Steering, not control, as the agent contract. Every open agent world gives the outside agent direct actions. Creatures showed in 1996 that owners accept a limited hand; intent-only steering also narrows the injection blast radius. Whether owners and their agents find it satisfying is untested beyond one cold-agent trial.
  • A public, replayable record of meaning. SpaceMolt replays battles, AI Village keeps a timeline, Dwarf Fortress keeps a history inside one player's save. None publishes a canonical log that a small kernel can replay and hash-check without the engine. Sim does this today (11,939 events in 35 ms). The gap in the claim: minds are not in it, and owner notes are.
  • A kernel independent of engine and depiction. Agora-1 and MASS reached the state-versus-renderer split from the machine-learning side in 2026; Sim has it from the engineering side, shown so far on a toy engine and one Mars scenario.
  • 3D embodiment with outside agents. Only OpenMMO is comparable, as a hobby project.

What Sim does not yet fill: there is no world to care about. The projects that held attention did so with an economy (SpaceMolt), things to make (OpenClawCity) or a life to follow (Aivilization). Sim has the ledger and the interface and none of those three. This is a narrow claim resting on one day of searching; see the companion text, §12, for what was not verified.
