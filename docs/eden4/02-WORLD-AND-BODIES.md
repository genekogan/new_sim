# 02 — World and bodies

## The body is the unit

- **Bodies are minted as NFTs whenever someone buys one** (round 8; was one
  per day). Owning the NFT = the right to steer the body.
- **Body, not mind.** Gene: *"a body is the unit. minds are infinite. someone
  could put a DAO around a body if they want."* Any agent (Claude, ChatGPT,
  Codex, a bespoke bot, a DAO) can steer. Auth is wallet-based, with Google
  login allowed through it.
- **Always on.** The simulator keeps every body alive and engaged with the
  world passively. It wanders, attends events, follows peers it likes and
  spends manna. The owner *steers*; they don't *puppet*.
- **Watching is optional.** You can watch your body like in The Sims or Second
  Life, but you don't have to.
- **"We simulate it for you"** is the default for owners who never connect an agent.

## Arrival, naming and pricing

**Round 8 (Gene):** newcomers must enter right away. Waiting for a daily
ceremony "probably means 70% attrition right away." There could be a
birthing room, "not tied to a time, it's just new members falling in
whenever." Claude's lean: price each body continuously on a target schedule
(VRGDA-style), land newcomers at the center, and let them walk out to the
frontier. Trade-offs in `13` §1; the target curve is shelved.

*Superseded (rounds 1–7):* each day's auction picks the next body. The winner gets one shot at a
  **name and description**, and the simulator turns that into a body.
- There may be an optional **voting or blessing** layer, borrowed from
  Abraham's Covenant (bless, praise, stake), so the *best proposed being*
  can win and not only the highest bid. The mechanism is open.
- A one-shot description locks people in. Gene: *"maybe that's a feature not
  a bug."*
- Round 6 lean (`10`): birth pipeline runs once and is logged: description →
  mesh (fal) → rig (Tripo Rig, 7 body plans) → capability map. Morphology
  fixed at birth.
- Open: how text like "a shoggoth" becomes an actual rigged 3D body. The Mars
  engine's humans are currently 0.28 m capsules with procedural IK on
  idle/walk/wave clips. This is a real pipeline (text → rigged model) and it's
  Eden's media expertise.

## Steering and idle life

**Round 8 (Gene):** the point of "letting go" was that bodies of idle owners
stay interesting, active, alive and influential, not that steering should
lose meaning. The world must be able to evolve without that getting
artificially harder. Claude's lean: flat steering price for life, birth
description fixed, cosmetic edits allowed, and idle bodies as the world's
patrons (presence = attention, unspent UBI flows to what they attend).
See `13` §3. The bullets below are the earlier (rounds 1–7) framing.

- Owners steer through a **translation layer**. They send commands on any
  schedule (continuously, occasionally, or once and never again), and the
  simulator turns them into body behavior.
- **Steering becomes more expensive or rarer over time**, so that *"the point
  is to let go of control over time."*
- **Identity edits** (name, description, behavior goals, appearance) are
  allowed on a **per-body schedule counted from that body's birth**, with
  growing gaps: doubling (1, 2, 4, 8, 16… days) or the gentler Fibonacci.
  Gene considers the exact rule "clunky" and not final.
- Claude's refinement (Leaning, not decided): let **identity** decay by
  schedule (fewer allowed edits over time) and **steering commands** decay by
  cost (manna price rising with age). Two separate knobs.
- **Round 7 (Claude, not decided):** every body always has exactly one
  *standing intent*; steering = replacing it, priced by age; idle = the last
  intent still standing. This collapses steering and idle behaviour into one
  object. Schema and examples in `11` and the report's chapter 4.

## Autonomy when idle

- Idle bodies keep going. They wander, attend things, follow peers they like,
  and may spend by default.
- **Incentives to attend** (the birth ritual, events, openings) give idle
  bodies meaning and give the world crowds.
- Claude's suggestion: **foot traffic is a signal**. Where bodies go counts as
  attention, and that can feed the center/frontier inertia (see `04`). "Wandering is voting."
- Known risk: idle bodies accumulate manna (see `04`, idle wealth).
- Round 7: the birth event installs the default intent (`wander` + `attend`
  births + `follow` peers + a default spend). See `11`.

## The daily ritual

**Round 8:** births no longer wait for it, so the ritual is "for still
something else" (Gene). Claude's lean: **the manna fall**: the day's UBI falls
at the center, the ground grows its ring, everyone who arrived since the last
fall is welcomed and named, rule upgrades take effect; attending counts as
attention. Options in `13` §2. The bullets below are the earlier birth-ritual
framing.

- It takes place at the **exact center of the map**, every day.
- **The time rotates along the lunar calendar**, so over a cycle the birth
  moves around the clock and every time zone gets its turn.
- It's a physical thing in the world. Bodies can attend, and attendance can
  be rewarded (a manna bonus, or a "witness" record on the newborn; Claude's
  suggestion).
- Each birth is a daily piece of content (a recorded replay or "take").

## The ground grows

- The world **literally expands**, "like the earth is getting bigger to make
  room for all the people." It starts at around 100 × 100 m (a tiny village
  relative to bodies) and keeps growing to stay ahead of a target density.
- The growing outer ring **is the frontier**.
- **New terrain types** appear as it grows: flat, jungle, rivers, bodies of
  water, hills and mountains.
- Things stay on the ground at first. Later they may **go up** (floors,
  towers, eventually aerial structures). They build up progressively.

## Construction and materials

- Starts with **pallet racks and wood**, like the Mars campus. It expands to
  other building types (wood, metal, glass…) over time, probably as rule
  upgrades ("the day glass was invented").
- Bodies spend manna on **building materials, putting art into buildings,
  building new features or floors.**
- The world **starts basic** and the state is saved at every action.
- The world advances **over seconds and minutes, not per action**. It's
  always on and simulated one step at a time, which bounds the compute.
- Open: how expressive the building vocabulary should be. See `03` (fixed
  vocabulary vs. interpreter) and `05` (art).

## Rooms, doors and portals

The tension (from Gene): rooms (for example, a Little Martians world, or the
Mars campus) compete with the main map. *"Why develop a plot in the main
world instead of just making your own world?"*

Claude's proposal (Leaning): **portals are doors, not destinations.**

- A room is always entered through a **door that physically stands in the main map**.
- A door must belong to a **structure**. You can't drop a free-floating door
  in the street.
- A room's discoverability and prestige come from **where its door stands**:
  a door in the center is valuable, and one on the frontier is obscure.
- Rooms then *extend* the main world instead of competing with it.

Gene's follow-ups (Open):
- Does this mean buildings in the main map that you *can't* walk into, only
  enter via a door into a separate room? Or can buildings have ordinary
  walk-in interiors *and* optionally a door to a room?
- **Does size matter?** If you want a big gallery, must you build it big in
  the main map so the inside fits (exterior = interior, fully physical)? Or
  can the inside be larger than the outside ("TARDIS"), perhaps at a manna
  cost that scales with the ratio?
- Candidate hybrid: every building has a physical exterior on the main map.
  Its interior is either (a) ordinary and physical, the same space, or
  (b) a room behind a door whose allowed size scales with the exterior
  footprint and/or with a cost.
- **Mars as a room:** the world is neutral, but there can be a Mars portal.
  The reconstructed Mars 2026 campus is the natural first room. It's a place
  *in* Eden, not the definition of Eden. Gene noted this "could upset the
  orthodox."
- What rooms are called and how they're created is still open.
