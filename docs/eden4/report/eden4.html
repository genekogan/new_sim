1 · The thesis

A world born one citizen at a time. New bodies drop into a single shared, centrally simulated world whenever someone buys one, and a daily ritual at the center keeps the world's clock. The world is made collectively from the actions of all the bodies. Owners give them high-level direction when they want to, and the simulator keeps them alive and influential when they don't.

It collapses Gene's projects into one thing: Eden (the platform, agents, media generation) is the world itself. Manna is the money. Mars supplies the engine and the first room. The Sims, Second Life and Decentraland give the inhabited persistent world. Nouns DAO gives NFT-owned identity and the treasury. Abraham's Covenant gives the daily ritual.

I think of The Sims, Decentraland and Second Life as artworks all the same. It is what it is. It is Eden.Gene, round 2

Principles that keep recurring

  • Alive without you. Steering is optional; an idle owner's body still feels alive and influential. (Earlier "letting go", control fading over time; Gene pulled back in round 8: the world must stay easy to evolve, not artificially harder.)
  • Shape now, numbers later. Design the kinds of things; let the early community reveal the values.
  • Canonical history. The past never changes. Upgrades are events in history.
  • The artifact should live alone. Neutral, and able to survive its creator.
  • Few rules. One elegant rule that yields a continuum beats several special cases.

2 · World and bodies

The body is the unit

Withdrawn One body minted per day. Decided Newcomers enter right away. Bodies are NFTs; owning the NFT is the right to steer the body. A body is the unit; minds are infinite. Any agent can steer: Claude, ChatGPT, Codex, a bespoke bot, a DAO. Auth is wallet-based with Google login through it.

Decided Bodies are always on. The simulator keeps every body alive passively: it wanders, attends events, follows peers it likes, spends manna. The owner steers, they do not puppet. Watching is optional. "We simulate it for you" is the default for owners who never connect an agent.

Arrival and naming

A problem with the birth ceremony is it means someone who just discovered this and wants to play right away can't. … This probably means 70% attrition right away. I think you have to enter right away. There could be a birthing room but it's not tied to a time, it's just new members falling in whenever.Gene, round 8

Leaning Each body is priced continuously on a target schedule (VRGDA-style): price rises when births run ahead of the target and falls when behind. Instant arrival, one growth knob; the target curve is Shelved. Newcomers land at the center and walk outward (Open).

The owner gets one shot at a name and description ("a shoggoth", "a skateboarder girl with a green hat", "the muse bot"), and the simulator turns that into a body. A blessing or voting layer borrowed from the Covenant is Open. The one-shot lock-in is, in Gene's words, "maybe a feature not a bug."

Steering and idle life

The thing that I was really trying to solve … is a way of making bodies controlled by idle owners still somehow interesting and active. That steering is optional and that without steering the character still feels alive and influential somehow.Gene, round 8

Withdrawn Steering gets harder over time. Leaning Flat steering price for life (a rate limit for fairness only). The birth description stays fixed; cosmetic edits are allowed. Idle bodies are the world's patrons: their presence counts as attention, and their unspent UBI flows by default to what they attend or follow. That makes them influential and also solves idle wealth.

The daily ritual

Decided One ritual a day at the exact center, at a time that rotates along the lunar calendar. Births no longer wait for it. Leaning It becomes the manna fall: the day's UBI falls at the center, the ground grows its ring, everyone who arrived since the last fall is welcomed and named, rule upgrades take effect. Attending counts as attention, not income.

The ground grows

Decided The world literally expands, "like the earth is getting bigger to make room for all the people." The outer ring is the frontier. New terrain types appear as it grows. Ground first, height later.

Rooms, doors and portals

Leaning Portals are doors, not destinations. A room is entered through a door that stands on a structure in the main map, so a room's prestige comes from where its door is. Gene's follow-ups are Open: walk-in interiors versus door-rooms, whether interiors can be larger than exteriors, what rooms are called. Mars 2026 as the first room; a Mars portal "could upset the orthodox."

3 · One simulator, many verifiers

Decided One central simulator, not a decentralized one, because competing simulators contradict each other. The simulator is not on-chain, so it can be experimented with. The record of the world is.

The interpreter debate

Claude first proposed an interpreter: a model call Eden runs inside the simulator, acting as game master when an agent asks for something outside the physics vocabulary. Gene's objection is the one that shaped everything after:

An interpreter conflicts with the world being like a blockchain, a sequence of actions run through a pre-built compiler, and with credible neutrality. What happens if the interpreter goes away? The artifact must be able to live alone.Gene, round 4 (paraphrased in the log)

The alternative he sketched, a locked simulator with a fixed building vocabulary, brought its own fear: not being able to upgrade, or getting the initial spec wrong.

Replayable and upgradeable

Decided in principle Immutability and upgradeability are compatible if every rule set stays runnable forever and upgrades are events in history. Every action carries the rules version in force. Old versions are frozen and content-addressed. An upgrade is a logged event at tick N with a logged migration. Mistakes are fixed forward, never retroactively.

A future-proof rule set that replays the world from t=0 with rules as they evolve. So the universe itself can evolve.Gene, round 4

The rollup analogy

Leaning Eden is the sequencer. The input log (or its commitments) and periodic state roots go on-chain. Anyone can verify by replaying with the open, versioned simulator. If Eden stops, the log, rule bundles and assets are public and permanent, so anyone can reconstruct and continue. Who is allowed to sequence next is Open (Q2). Gene, round 6: questions 1 to 3 remain open and are fairly non-urgent.

4 · Three layers

Round 7 · Claude's refinement of Gene's "WorldPrompt split in two" · Leaning

Runway's WorldPrompt (September 2026) is a genesis prompt plus a timestamped stream of free-text events addressed to a subject or the scene, resolved by a video model into pixels. Gene's instinct: keep that input shape, but let a game engine, not a video model, produce the result, and split it into an AI outer layer that produces intents and a deterministic controller that resolves them into the next state.

The refinement: it is three layers, and the middle one is where the design lives. The question Gene asked, how can the middle layer be deterministic when its inputs are mushy natural language?, has a short answer: the middle layer never sees natural language. Language stops at the edge.

Why intents, not verbs, are the unit

  • Bodies are always on; owners are mostly absent. One standing intent keeps a body alive for a year on a single message. A verb stream would need a model at the center for every idle body.
  • The log stays small and human-readable. "4102: follow Kalama" instead of a million walk_tos.
  • Neutrality. The center never interprets language. The owner's agent translates; the center validates a schema.
  • Replay. Verbs are recomputed from intents and state, so they never need storage and cannot drift.

The intent is small and closed: a goal from a short list, a target id, bounded params, an until, and a fallback. There is no free text field. If an owner's agent wants to say something the schema can't hold, that is the schema's limit and the agent's problem, and extending the goal list is a rules upgrade, a public event, not a private judgment call.

Worked examples

Example A · A steered body

Owner says
"Have Mycos follow Kalama around. If she's busy, go garden by the river."
Their agent emits
{ kind: "intent", subject: 4102, author: 0x9a…f1,
  payload: { goal: "follow", target: {body: 12}, until: null,
    fallback: { goal: "tend", target: {region: "river-east"}, fallback: null } } }
Sequencer
Checks the signature owns body 4102, the schema is valid, body 12 and region river-east exist, and charges the steering price for a body this age. Assigns tick 88,412. Appends.
Tick 88,412
Policy reads body 12's state: activity = converse, which the policy's table calls busy. Falls through to tend. Finds the nearest tendable noun in river-east. Emits walk_to(plot 771).
Tick 88,430
Arrived. Emits tend(plot 771). Engine advances the plant's growth counter and debits the tending cost in manna.
Tick 89,004
Body 12 stands up and walks. Policy: no longer busy, primary goal resumes. Emits walk_to two metres behind Kalama.
Replay
Anyone running the same pinned policy and engine over the same log gets the same three verbs at the same ticks and the same state root. Nothing was stored except the intent.

Example B · An owner who never comes back

Birth event
Installs the default intent: wander in the body's home region, with two standing overrides every body has, attend(birth_ritual) when one is scheduled and follow a peer it has spent time near. Nothing else ever arrives from this owner.
Year 3
The body is still walking to births, still trailing the same two friends, still spending its UBI by the default spend rule. It has never cost the center a model call. It is a citizen.

Example C · Weather is an intent too

Governance key emits
{ kind: "intent", subject: "scene", author: ritual-contract,
  payload: { goal: "weather", params: {rain: 0.7, wind: 0.3}, until: {tick: 91,000} } }
Engine
Sets env.weather for the duration. Bodies' policies read it: gather prefers roofed regions when rain is high. No new mechanism, just a second kind of subject and a rule about who may address it (Q23).

Example D · High-framerate steering

A human at a keyboard
Their agent replaces the intent thirty times a minute: go here, go there, perform(wave). Each replacement is a separate signed event with its own tick and its own price. A per-body rate limit keeps it fair (Q26). (Rounds 6–7 priced replacements by the body's age; round 8 withdrew that.)
What Runway binds to keys
Premade action prompts. Here the same thing is a premade intent bound to a key. Same interface, but the result is state, not pixels.

Example E · The day glass was invented

Rules event
{ kind: "rules", tick: 2,140,000, version: 7,
  bundle: sha256(engine+policy+schemas), migration: sha256(m6→7) }
What changed
The affordance schema gains a transparent material tag and the support rule learns glass's chain cap. No new verb: install_panel already existed. Replay runs v6 to that tick, applies the migration, continues with v7. The bug in v6's rafter spacing stays in history.

Example F · Something the schema can't say

Owner says
"Start a religion around the old kiln."
Their agent
Has to compose it from what exists: gather at the kiln daily, perform, converse with whoever attends, place a media piece on the kiln's face. If several bodies adopt similar intents, that is a religion, emergently, and the log will show exactly when it started. The center never had to know the word.
Round 8 note
Claude's revised reading: the schema is too small in one place only, time. "Daily at dawn" can't recur with a single until. Proposal (Open): a recurrence slot on the intent (daily, lunar), the same schedule the ritual uses. And aggregates are derived, never declared: "the old kiln" is a support-connected set of pieces; the congregation is the bodies whose intents share that target.

The policy's future

Version one is hand-written: priorities, pathing, attendance, simple sociality. The Mars engine's ambient_wander, gather, converse and campfire_circle are its seeds. A learned policy (intent plus local state to verb) can replace it later as a rules upgrade, if it is deterministic and pinned: fixed weights, reproducible inference. That is the honest route to a "world model player" that never breaks replay.

5 · The log and the fold

Round 7 · Claude · Leaning

Five event kinds, totally ordered, signed by authors, ticked by the sequencer.

Event = { seq, tick, kind, author, payload, sig }
kind  ∈ { birth, intent, ingest, econ, rules }

birth   name, description, body asset hashes (mesh, rig, capability map), sale price
intent  subject (body | scene), goal, target, params, until, fallback
ingest  a new noun: GLB hash + affordances + license; or a media hash
econ    price set, buyout, tip, grant, treasury action
rules   "from tick N, rules vK": bundle hash + migration hash

The world is a fold over that log:

S_0      = genesis(rules v1)
I_t      = standing intents after events with tick ≤ t
verbs_t  = P_k(S_t, I_t)                     policy, version k in force
S_{t+1}  = E_k(S_t, verbs_t, events_t)       engine: verbs + births, ingests, econ
root_t   = hash(S_t)                         committed every N ticks

rng lives inside S, so the fold is pure. A rules event at tick N applies its migration to S_N and switches P and E. Replay from t=0 with pinned versions reproduces every root.

The sequencer's job

  1. Accept, validate and order events; assign ticks.
  2. Run the fold continuously. This is the background tick the Mars engine lacks.
  3. Commit roots; publish the log, rule bundles and assets to permanent storage.
  4. Serve state to renderers.

If Eden stops, anyone with the log, bundles and assets can do all four. Who is allowed to is Q2.

New question (Q26). When many agents steer at high rate, does the sequencer order by arrival, by a per-body rate limit, or by price?

6 · Representation

Round 6 · Claude · Leaning · Gene: "I agree with you" on the input shape; the rest not yet endorsed

Nouns open, verbs closed

The Mars engine already has the right split; it just needs the nouns pried out of TypeScript.

  • Verbs are the fixed, versioned vocabulary. Few, physical, goal-shaped: walk_to, sit, carry, connect, install, place, paint, gather, converse. A new verb (swim, fly, dig) is a rules upgrade and should stay rare and ceremonial.
  • Nouns are open data. An asset definition becomes a content-addressed record: a GLB hash plus affordances (footprint, support type, sockets, seats, media surfaces, grip slots, material tags). Anyone can ingest one. The engine validates affordances and never judges the mesh. A chair is a def with a seat. A projector is a def with a media surface. A greenhouse is a kit. Screens, podiums, lamps, plants and atriums cost zero rules.
  • Pieces are the unit of state. Every placed instance has an author, a birth tick, a material, a pose and a support. Buildings, rooms and districts are names at the edges, not state.
  • Media is a noun instance. An image, video, audio or 3D hash on a surface-bearing def. A painting on a facade and the wall behind it are governed by the same rule.
  • Physics is support only, never force. A piece stands if it is connected to ground through a chain of supports (Teardown's rule; the engine's support and connections fields already encode most of it). Materials may cap the chain (Valheim's rule). Disconnected islands are rejected at placement, not simulated falling. No mass, force, energy or gravity.

Characters: Spore at the level of verbs

A body is a rigged GLB of any topology plus a capability map: which verbs its rig can perform and how (IK targets, seat height, carry points). Spore's real trick (Hecker, 2008) was authoring animation against abstract goals and retargeting by IK at runtime. The engine's verbs are already goal-shaped, so retargeting is the renderer's problem, and the state only records that body 4102 is sitting on chair 88.

Birth pipeline, run once, logged. Description to mesh (Hunyuan3D, Tripo or Trellis on fal) to rig (Tripo Rig covers biped, quadruped, hexapod, octopod, avian, serpentine and aquatic) to capability map. Hashes go in the birth event. Morphology is fixed at birth; features, colours and accessories are identity edits on the decaying schedule. Unowned fauna and spirits use the same machinery with a standing intent and no owner.

Seed library

The "thingiverse dataset" is the Sketchfab catalog at /sketchfab/: 3,706 GLBs, 77.7 GB, in 25 lore categories (creatures 359, robots 207, devices 471, settings 397, nature 261, statues 411, objects 798, flora, eden-dome, and more), each with a written description, use tags, a scale tier and a license. About 2,960 are cc0 or by. Licensing for a public world with an economy is Q25.

Open from this chapter: Q21 the minimal affordance schema; Q22 the minimal intent goal list; Q24 whether births are limited to what can be rigged today or may supply any rigged GLB.

7 · Economy and property

This is something we'll have to design as we go. Upgradeable is good here. We have to make a society.Gene, round 3

Manna

Decided Every body receives manna daily as UBI and spends it on materials, art, features, floors and steering. Open ERC-20 or in-game? Claude leans in-game first, balances in committed state, bodies as the on-chain NFTs, a bridge later. Gene: "maybe the whole game can revolve around NFTs. Maybe this is the comeback of NFTs."

Idle wealth. Idle bodies earn UBI and spend little, so old idle bodies become the richest. Gene agrees this must not happen. Leaning (round 8): idle bodies as patrons, their unspent UBI flowing by default to what they attend or follow. UBI rate and demurrage are Shelved.

Center and frontier

Decided The center hardens and grows rich; the frontier stays poor and dynamic. Leaning One rule produces the gradient: cost to change a thing = base × age × attention, where attention is measured foot traffic and blessings. Wandering is voting.

What is a thing, and who may write to it

Gene was very interested in Harberger taxes for some kind of rights, but not on parcels. Claude proposed Harberger on protection rights over things, with price 0 as commons. Gene challenged it in round 5: protection makes sense for a painting on a facade, but who decides whether to add a room or whether it's glass, metal or wood? This is Q14b, the unit-of-a-thing problem.

Round 6 proposal, unanswered The piece is the only unit. Every piece has a price, the inertia formula. Paying it transfers authorship, after which you may alter or remove it, and the fee goes to the previous author, so overwriting someone's beloved wall means buying it from them. Harberger shrinks to one optional term: a self-assessed premium on top, taxed. Adding a supported piece next to or onto another is placing, not altering, and is never blocked. So protection never means "nothing touches this", only "nothing here changes without paying me." Gene has not responded to this yet.

Treasury

Decided Funded by body sales, it pays the team and makes grants. The loop: sales, treasury, UBI and grants, a better world, more valuable bodies, sales. Governance model is Open.

8 · Creation and moderation

Decided Art from the edges, Moltbook-style: owners' agents carry skills that make work and submit it; the author spends their own compute. Rejected "Programs, not pixels." Gene: an unnecessary constraint, combining ideas for their own sake. Kling must be admissible. Leaning Media is media, ingested and content-addressed, never hotlinked, so nothing can vanish after placement. poop is one optional skill, not a format.

10 · Engine frontier

Round 7 · researched 2026-09-29 · Claude · everything here is a menu, not a plan

Gene's second frontier: the actual engine, "simulation, camera, rendering, NPCs, modeling, assets, physics, and other advanced features that are in mature versions of this and can work on the internet." What follows is what mature engines and 2025–26 research make possible in a browser-first, server-canonical world, grouped by his list. Each item says what it would give eden4. Marks: have means the Mars engine already does it, missing means it is needed and absent, later means a later era. Items marked [U] rest on a single unverified source.

Simulation

  • Always-on world step. missing A Durable Object alarm or a dedicated sequencer process running the fold continuously. Cloudflare's limits as of June 2026 (10 GB SQLite per object, thousands of hibernating WebSockets, unlimited objects) suit one object per region shard plus one for the log. Shard on space, never on session.
  • Deterministic lockstep, proven at scale. Factorio runs the whole simulation on every client and syncs inputs only; Age of Empires II DE pins strict floating point. WebAssembly's IEEE-754 is strictly deterministic; TypeScript number math is too if the engine avoids transcendental Math.* drift (fixed-point or its own libm). have in spirit: the Mars engine is byte-identical across Node and workerd.
  • Mass entities. Unreal's MassEntity plus StateTree plus Smart Objects (5.5–5.8) run thousands of background agents as integers with LOD'd representation and reservation-based activities. This is the blueprint for ten thousand bodies where a handful are actively steered. The Mars engine's regions and reservedBy fields are the seed; crowd scale (30 walkers ≈ 200 ms) is missing.
  • Time dilation. EVE Online slows the simulation clock under load rather than dropping inputs. A world that advances in seconds and minutes can borrow this for free: the tick rate is a rule, not a promise.
  • A storyteller. RimWorld's AI Storyteller, descended from Left 4 Dead's director, paces events per day. In our terms it is a governance-keyed scene-intent author. The daily ritual already is one such beat.

Camera and cinematography

  • Virtual cameras with priority blends. Unity Cinemachine 3.1's Follow, FreeLook, spline dolly and obstacle avoidance model is portable to three.js. have a camera vocabulary (orbit, dolly, crane, fly_through) and natural-language camera sentences via parse_camera.
  • An AI director of photography. GenDoP (ICCV 2025) and PACE (2026-09) emit typed shot specs from scene semantics. An edge agent can be the DoP: it writes shot specs, the engine executes them deterministically, so every viewer can watch their own cut of the same canonical day.
  • Replays as state, not video. Rocket League's replays are input scripts with an AI-chosen director camera. A replayable log makes a free television channel of the world: the daily birth as a broadcast, any past day re-shot from any angle. have takes and the "watch it being built" player.

Rendering

  • three.js WebGPURenderer with TSL is the default path. r186 (2026-09-08) adds cascaded sun shadows and async compute; WebGPU is universal since Safari 26. One TypeScript codebase, compute shaders for culling and crowds, WebGL2 fallback only for low-end devices. have three.js; missing WebGPU.
  • GPU-driven instancing and impostors. A 2026 three.js demo places roughly 190,000 trees and 450,000 understory instances by GPU scatter, culls per frame into indirect draws, and bakes octahedral impostors for the far field (two triangles per instance). This is how millions of user-ingested nouns stay visible at the horizon. Bake the impostor atlas at ingest.
  • Virtualized geometry. A Nanite-style port exists on WebGPU (meshlet hierarchy, software rasterizer, per-meshlet culling) but is heavy; the production path is a meshopt LOD ladder plus impostors. Claims of "Nanite 3.0 for WebGPU" [U].
  • Dynamic global illumination. Radiance Cascades run on WebGPU compute; Split Radiance Cascades (2026-07) give 3D diffuse bounce from sparse world-space probes. A world that changes every day cannot bake light; this gives time-of-day bounce without baking. MegaLights (UE 5.8) shows stochastic many-light sampling is the target for a city of lamps.
  • Gaussian splats next to meshes. Spark 2.0 (World Labs, 2026-04) streams continuous-LOD splat trees with a 16M-splat GPU paging pool in the same three.js scene as meshes. PlayCanvas SuperSplat 3.0 (2026-06) streams up to 24M Gaussians on compute. Photogrammetry and Marble-generated worlds become ground and backdrop under rigged GLB bodies.
  • Asset pipeline at ingest. gltfpack with meshopt, KTX2/Basis textures (ETC1S for colour, UASTC for normals), a LOD chain via meshoptimizer, per-def face and texture budgets. missing. Decentraland's publish-then-LOD pipeline is the model.

NPCs, characters, animation

  • Motion matching per rig class. Stock in Unreal since 5.3, productized by Motorica. Ship a motion database per body plan and get locomotion quality without hand-authored blend trees. missing; people are capsules with renderer poses.
  • Motion for arbitrary topologies. AnyTop (any-topology diffusion, 2025), Motion2Motion cross-topology transfer (2025-08), MotionBricks real-time modular latent motions (2026-04), STyMo few-shot style transfer (2026-09). This is what makes "a rigged GLB of any topology" walk, sit and carry. Generate clips at birth, store them with the body.
  • Physics-based control (score-matching priors, whole-body tracking, 2025) is not browser-feasible yet. later, server-side or precomputed.
  • Faces. TalkingHead.js drives ARKit visemes on Mixamo rigs in the browser; Audio2Face outputs 52 ARKit blendshapes. Bodies that carry ARKit shapes can speak with faces.
  • Behaviour: StateTree and Smart Objects. Nouns advertise affordances as claimable objects (a bench offers sit, a kiln offers gather), which maps one-to-one onto the affordance schema in chapter 6 and onto MCP tools. GOAP or utility planners are the cheap deterministic layer under the intent: the edge sets the goal, the planner executes. This is the policy of chapter 4, and it is what shipping LLM-NPC games (inZOI, Where Winds Meet, Convai) converged on: the model for intent and dialogue, schedules for everything else.
  • Schedules, proven. Smallville's daily-to-hourly-to-minute schedules with memory and reflection, and Project Sid's thousand agents in Minecraft with emergent roles and norms. Tick-indexed schedules keep this deterministic.
  • Need-driven fauna. Rain World's creatures pursue their own survival; RimWorld keeps its ecology "observable and comprehensible." Unowned creatures with standing intents give a living world at no model cost.

Modeling and assets

  • Generated nouns with behaviour schemas. Roblox Cube (open source, 2025-03) then its 4D generation beta (2026-02): text to functional objects with behaviour schemas, 160,000 player-generated objects in one experience, an open-vocabulary schema system, and CubePart (2026-05) for part-controllable generation. This is the closest existing thing to "a noun with affordances" generated in one prompt.
  • Rigged bodies on demand. Meshy auto-rigs humanoid and quadruped with 600+ preset clips; Tripo Rig covers seven body plans; Hunyuan3D is open but static. See chapter 9.
  • Procedural layout. Wave Function Collapse and model synthesis remain the standard for tiled districts (Townscaper, Bad North, Caves of Qud); a 2025 WFC plus genetic-algorithm hybrid reports 73% fewer unplayable layouts. Deterministic and seedable inside the engine, useful for frontier terrain and starter streets.
  • Procedural terrain on the GPU. Height, biome and erosion generators exist for three.js at planet scale. The expanding ground can be generated per day from the day's seed. missing.
  • Editor-side generation is mainstream. Unity AI (open beta 2026-05) and Unreal 5.8 ship LLM and MCP editor workflows. Validates MCP as the editing surface. have.

Physics

  • Support graphs, not rigid bodies. Valheim: a foundation touching ground is 100% supported, decaying per hop, colour-coded. Teardown: anything still connected to ground stands. Graph propagation, cheap and deterministic. have most of it in support/connections; material chain caps missing.
  • Deterministic rigid bodies when needed. Rapier (WASM, cross-platform determinism flag, 2025 voxel colliders and persistent islands, SIMD builds 2–5× faster, GPU rigid bodies on the 2026 roadmap). Box2D 3.1 is deterministic across platforms; Box3D was announced 2026-06-30, alpha, no WASM yet. Jolt in WASM has soft bodies, cloth and vehicles for client-only cosmetics. later, and only server-side canonical.

Audio

  • Spatial audio. Web Audio's HRTF panner is still the only native spatializer; Resonance is dormant. Adequate for ambience; an ambisonic AudioWorklet if VR matters.
  • A daily-born score. Suno v5.5 (2026-03, continuation and personas), Stable Audio 3, ElevenLabs Music, and Magenta RealTime for adaptive scores. Precompute stems per biome and time of day, crossfade by region. A world born one body a day can have music born one day at a time, generated at the edges and ingested like any media.
  • Media playback on surfaces (screens, projectors, speakers as nouns) is missing in the engine; the legacy room layer has seven gallery screens.

Environment, weather, time

  • Volumetric sky on three.js WebGPU. Eanpa-Sky (weather fronts, lightning, day/night), Three.js Sky Pro (scattering, clouds, god rays, cloud shadows), three-clouds (Beer shadow maps). Server-canonical env.weather have drives client volumetrics; cloud shadows tie the sky to the simulation.
  • Seasons and foliage states. Unreal 5.8's vegetation authoring is the reference for season-driven foliage. A lunar-calendar ritual suggests a lunar season system for free.

Multiplayer and networking

  • Transport. WebTransport is in every browser since Safari 26.4 (2026-03). Unreliable datagrams for presence, WebSockets for canonical events. Colyseus 0.18 (2026-08) adds server-authoritative prediction and rollback if human-driven bodies ever need it.
  • Interest management and sharding. The ACM survey remains the reference; Hadean's Aether Engine partitioned space for 10,412 participants (EVE Aether Wars, 2019); SpatialOS shipped Worlds Adrift and then collapsed in the 2019 Unity dispute. The lesson across all of them: cross-shard entity handoff is the hard part. Keep shard boundaries static and coarse (center, rings), hand off by deterministic tick. later, needed around a thousand bodies.
  • Streaming. Clients load only chunks near their camera with LOD. missing, needed past a few hundred nouns.

Persistence and creator economies

  • What retention actually is. Fortnite's UEFN paid creators $1B by 2026-06 and 47% of playtime is in creator islands; Roblox paid over $1B in 2025 and moved to Creator Rewards. In-world items and creator share are the retention lever. Manna UBI plus a creator share of buyouts (chapter 7) is the same shape.
  • Onchain worlds. Lattice's Redstone chain shut down 2026-05-15; MUD and Dojo continue. The lesson for us: keep the chain for ownership and verification, run the simulation off-chain, publish roots for replay-verifiability. This is exactly the rollup posture of chapter 3.
  • Decentraland SDK 7.29 (2026-09-15) with a Unity Explorer client, Worlds without the Genesis vertical limit, background LOD generation on publish. A peer, and a pipeline to copy.

What I would take from this list first

  1. The always-on step and the signed log, because nothing else is real without them.
  2. The ingest pipeline (gltfpack, KTX2, LOD chain, affordance validation, impostor bake), because nouns-open depends on it.
  3. Motion databases per body plan plus any-topology motion transfer, because bodies are the product.
  4. Smart-object affordances as the bridge between nouns and the policy.
  5. Everything under rendering can wait until there is something to look at; three.js WebGPU makes most of it a later upgrade rather than a rewrite.

Unverified: "Nanite 3.0 for WebGPU", Unity 7 shipping WebGPU by default, and Cloudflare's "10 dynamic workers per Durable Object."

12 · Parameters and trade-offs

Round 8 · Gene's method, Claude's tables

I want to shelf some of the final decisions on some of these and possibly just let them happen organically depending on what early community likes. I don't need to design everything now.Gene, round 8

Fix the shape of the world: what kinds of things exist and how they change. Leave the numbers as dials, set by rule versions after watching what the early community does. Versioned rules make every dial change a logged event, never a retroactive fix, so shelving is cheap. Where a dial has a generous and a strict end, v1 sits near generous: adding friction when something is abused is easier than removing friction nobody enjoys. And never make the future artificially harder: friction belongs to places and prices, not to the ability to change the rules.

13 · Ledger

Decided by Gene

  • A body is the unit; minds are infinite. Newcomers enter right away (round 8).
  • Bodies always on, simulated passively; owners steer from any agent on any schedule; wallet auth with Google login. Idle bodies must still feel alive and influential; the world must stay easy to evolve (round 8).
  • One central simulator. The world advances over seconds and minutes with bounded compute.
  • The ground expands outward from ~100 × 100 m; the frontier is the outer ring; new terrain as it grows.
  • One daily ritual at the center, time rotating with the lunar calendar (originally a birth ritual).
  • Daily manna UBI. Center hardens and grows rich; frontier stays poor and dynamic. Idle-rich must not happen.
  • Pallet racks and wood first; ground first, height later.
  • Immutability plus upgradeability via versioned rules replayed from t=0 (in principle).
  • World neutral; places and rooms exist; a Mars portal is possible. Treasury pays the team and makes grants.
  • Audience: friends, believers, speculators last. Art from the edges.
  • Input shape like WorldPrompt, produced by the game engine, not a video model (round 7, "I agree with you").

Withdrawn by Gene, round 8

  • One body per day, auctioned, ~30 years, ~10,000 bodies. It "artificially constrains growth in a very fundamental way, persistently and forever."
  • Controlling a body gets harder over time. The aim was aliveness when idle.

Leaning

  • Interpreter at the edges; a fixed, versioned core vocabulary. Rollup-style: Eden sequences, log and roots on-chain, permanent storage, anyone can verify and resume.
  • Three layers: edge intents, deterministic policy, engine. Intents are the logged unit. Five event kinds. Scene aspects are intents from governance keys.
  • Nouns open (asset defs with affordances), verbs closed. Pieces as the unit of state. Support-only physics. Bodies of any topology with a capability map, morphology fixed at birth.
  • Media is media, ingested and content-addressed. Moderation by geography plus a hard floor. Manna in-game first, bodies as NFTs.
  • Inertia: change cost = base × age × attention. Price-to-alter with fees to the author; Harberger as an optional premium.
  • Doors on structures in the main map. Mars 2026 as the first room.
  • Round 8 (Claude, not endorsed): VRGDA birth pricing; the manna fall as the ritual; flat steering; idle bodies as patrons; land at the center; derived aggregates and a recurrence slot on intents.

Shelved

Every number in chapter 12: birth target curve and any cap or end; UBI rate; demurrage; inertia coefficients; Harberger scope; manna on-chain; treasury split; verticality timing; identity-edit rules; auction vs. blessing.
