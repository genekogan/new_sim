# Simulation frontier applications: Mars and a broader Eden world

Owner discussion: Gene, 2026-09-30. Proposed design directions for future reference.
This document does not assert historical Mars events, model real people's private
relationships, or establish canon/requirements for Eden or Little Martians.
“Eden world” here means Gene's proposed larger world-design ambition; its setting,
inhabitants, scale and rules remain to be defined with him.
The reusable capabilities are described in [SIMULATION_FRONTIER.md](SIMULATION_FRONTIER.md).

## Distinguish three layers

**Mars application:** recognizable community situations, local setting, selected
objects, authored roles and supported routines. Hypothetical Lab scenarios can be
built directly without pretending they reconstruct a particular historical event.
Any named historical reconstruction still needs source provenance/content ownership.

**Reusable simulator:** projects, commitments, participation, relationships, object
capabilities, shared access, spatial coordination and persistent consequences.
Keep these independent of Mars names, campus layouts and particular meshes.

**Broader Eden world:** apply that simulator to multiple settings, communities and
possibly species, with additional institutions, travel and environmental systems
only when the chosen experiences require them. A larger map alone is not a richer world.

## Mars-shaped engagements and what transfers

All entries below are proposed hypothetical play experiences, not historical claims.

| Engagement | Mars application | General mechanism / other applications |
|---|---|---|
| Prepare a talk and meet afterward | Develop a demonstration, prepare the room, present, ask questions, continue a collaboration | Preparation, appointments, equipment, floor/attention, follow-up; conference or office |
| Shared art/technology project | Collaborators make an installation or prototype over several sessions | Intermediate products, tools, role negotiation, skill, handoff; workshop or laboratory |
| Communal dinner | Volunteers divide preparation, invite people, serve, socialize and clean | Task dependencies, supplies, hosting, cleanup; household, café or community centre |
| Build and maintain a temporary space | Arrange a usable space, repair an object, inspect equipment, pack it down | Shared work, support, material identity, staged progress; makerspace or field camp |
| Rehearsal and showcase | Practice, coordinate cues, prepare a venue, perform and receive feedback | Timing, learning, audiences, presentation; theatre, music studio or gallery |
| Welcome a newcomer | Tour useful places, introduce companions, help find a workspace | Knowledge sharing, familiarity, hosting, access; workplace or neighbourhood |
| Shared resource stewardship | Borrow tools, replenish supplies, charge equipment and maintain plants | Permissions, return obligations, availability, care; any shared facility |
| Excursion and return | Meet, pack, travel together, regroup and resume campus intentions | Group journeys, logistics, separation, continuity; town, park or expedition |

Mars supplies a strong first set of meanings and constraints. None of these core
mechanisms should require a Mars-specific implementation. Setting-specific assets,
content and activity preferences should be authored data where practical.

## More general ambitions beyond the immediate Mars tranche

- **Households and life histories:** private/shared spaces, moving home, domestic
  agreements, deeper relationships and optionally life stages. Consent and privacy
  matter if scenarios use identifiable real people; fictional casts are a clean default.
- **Occupations and services:** shops, cafés, clinics, schools, repair services,
  queues, appointments, roles and reputations. Money is optional until exchange needs it.
- **Institutions and collective decisions:** community responsibilities, proposals,
  allocating scarce space/equipment, resolving disagreements and changing rules.
- **Multiple connected places:** residences, streets, markets, transit, workshops,
  gardens, performance venues and natural areas. Travel has a purpose and preserves
  intentions across locations.
- **Ecology and infrastructure:** seasons, growth, water, energy, waste and maintenance
  with consequences where useful. Start with selective systems, not exhaustive realism.
- **Other species and embodiments:** species-appropriate movement, needs, perception
  and interaction. Dogs and any fictional inhabitants need their own supported models;
  neither should be human behavior with a different mesh.
- **Creator and player society:** author reusable objects and engagements, participate
  directly, share places and perhaps collaborate online. Multiplayer, economy and
  creator distribution are separate substantial choices, not assumed prerequisites.

## Places as activity systems

A kitchen offers preparation, storage, serving and cleaning. A workshop offers shared
tools, instruction and unfinished products. A gallery offers installation, attention
and hosting. A household offers privacy, possessions and routines. A garden offers
recurring care and growth. A transport hub offers departures, luggage and logistics.
Select places for the interactions they enable, not merely for scenery variety.

## Bridge from current work

Keep the released classroom and connected communal-day work moving. Then select one
project spanning multiple sessions and one modest alternative setting. A workshop
project followed by a gallery opening is a strong candidate: it connects work and
social life while testing that the framework generalizes without copying Mars logic.
Do not activate all directions in this document at once.

Before committing to a larger Eden tranche, interview Gene on: whether its primary
experience is directing a society, embodied participation or world authoring; the
first community and places; fictional versus historical inhabitants; desired time
scale; and which consequential systems matter first. These are future decisions,
not blockers for current Mars implementation.
