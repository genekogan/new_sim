# Simulation frontier: lives, projects and shared intentions

Owner discussion: Gene, 2026-09-30. Future-facing reference, not an implementation
claim, funded tranche, new active queue, or instruction to pause current work.
Read [SIMULATION_VISION.md](SIMULATION_VISION.md) for current product direction and
[ACTIVE_ROADMAP.md](ACTIVE_ROADMAP.md) for selected work. Applications and the
Mars/Eden distinction live in [SIMULATION_FRONTIER_APPLICATIONS.md](SIMULATION_FRONTIER_APPLICATIONS.md).

## Desired leap

Move from executing individual activities to a world where people sustain intentions
across hours and days, cooperate, remember encounters, and leave consequences.
A project can be interrupted, revised, handed over, abandoned or resumed. Its success
is not guaranteed, but participants and objects must remain coherent.

The world should support directed, autonomous and mixed participation. Gene can
initiate an engagement, take a role, change the environment, interrupt it, or watch
people interpret its goals. Authored situations supply intentions and constraints;
runtime produces feasible variations rather than replaying one rigid script.

## Longer-lived engagements

- **Projects:** make an artwork, repair equipment, prepare an exhibition, build an
  installation, conduct an experiment, rehearse a performance. Preserve intermediate
  products, materials, tools, collaborators, progress and next actions between sessions.
- **Shared commitments:** promise help, schedule a session, recruit participants,
  negotiate roles, decline or postpone, delegate, and recover from missed commitments.
- **Learning:** demonstrate, observe, attempt, receive feedback, practice, and later
  apply a skill. Different abilities should affect choices and outcomes, not merely speed.
- **Relationships:** familiarity, trust, mentorship, friendship, attraction, rivalry
  and boundaries can influence invitation, proximity, assistance and interruption.
  Begin with a small observable subset; avoid an all-purpose psychology simulation.
- **Care and stewardship:** cook, clean, maintain tools, replenish supplies, care for
  plants and animals. One person's contribution changes another person's opportunities.
- **Open-ended social life:** join and leave groups, host newcomers, exchange help,
  form recurring gatherings and maintain companions across activities.

## Continuity needed to make those engagements meaningful

1. Stable identities for people, objects, projects and commitments.
2. Intentions with a reason, priority, next step and explicit suspended/finished state.
3. Persistent physical world state: work products and belongings remain where validly
   placed; depleted, dirty, damaged or unfinished states change available actions.
4. Understandable shared access: permission, borrowing, exclusive use, temporary
   reservations, return obligations and handoff. A multi-day intention does not hold
   every resource exclusively for days.
5. Local knowledge: what a person has seen, learned or been told. Explain a missed
   appointment or misplaced tool without giving everyone omniscient knowledge.
6. Bounded adaptation: substitute only when permitted, renegotiate or wait with a
   reason, ask for intervention, or end and clean up. Preserve useful partial work.
7. Documented persistence across reloads and day boundaries. Fast-forward must not
   silently bypass physical transitions or change authoritative guarantees.

These are capability directions, not a demand to build a universal planning language.
Derive the minimum mechanisms from a working engagement; retain the deterministic
execution, ownership, support, collision and interruption rules of the current engine.
Routine behavior must not depend on continuous model calls.

## Social expression with a purpose

Make intentions readable through attention and timing: greet someone while working,
look toward a free seat, move aside for a newcomer, wait for an opening to speak,
yield the floor, hesitate, put down a cup before helping, or disengage politely.
Groups should split and merge; companions should regroup or separate gracefully.
Variation should follow context, personality and relationships rather than random
animation or frequent intention reselection. Generated dialogue is optional.

## Broader activity families

- Collaborative making and repair, teaching, critique and experimentation.
- Hosting, communal meals, cleaning, shared household responsibilities.
- Rehearsal, music, theatre, exhibitions and ceremonies.
- Board games, sports, dance and collective practice with actual state and timing.
- Excursions, packing, meeting, shared transport, deliveries and return journeys.
- Gardening, animal care, environmental adaptation and recurring maintenance.
- Longer-term occupations, exchange and institutions if a later world needs them.

## Candidate demonstrations

**Communal dinner:** supplies → preparation → invitations → serving → conversation
→ cleanup → interrupted work resumed. Different arrivals and unavailable equipment
should produce coherent variations.

**Collaborative workshop project:** a novice and experienced maker share tools,
learn, leave a partial artifact overnight, cope with a borrowed tool or absent
collaborator, and finish or revise the project the next day.

**Exhibition opening:** prepare and install work across sessions, test equipment,
assign host/presenter roles, welcome visitors, handle a failure, then dismantle or
retain the installation. Reuse the same mechanisms in a non-Mars gallery.

A later combined scenario might be: “Prepare an exhibition opening tonight.” It
should bind available people, places and equipment, preserve the author's intent,
and accept live intervention. This is an ambitious destination, not a current promise.

## Acceptance when a frontier becomes active

Show at least two configurations and a consequential disruption. Verify continuity
across sessions; no resource leaks; valid object ownership/support; coherent departure,
interruption and resumption; reproducibility under recorded decisions; understandable
waiting/refusal; and runtime captures showing readable social behavior. Distinguish
simulation correctness from bounded anatomical fidelity. Update the active roadmap
only when a specific supported increment is selected.

## Reference lenses

The Sims suggests individual needs, relationships, skills and life continuity;
Second Life suggests creator-authored places, usable objects and human participation;
GTA suggests embodied journeys and coordinated multi-stage missions. These are design
lenses, not claims that those games simulate every proposed capability or a mandate
to reproduce their scope.
