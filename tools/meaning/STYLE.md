# Writing useful world meaning

Name the place for a visitor who has never seen its build references. Say what people do there, one useful relation, and how its important parts differ. Preserve established proper names. Use compact natural prose, not furniture inventories or programming instructions. A ceramic cup needs at most its identity and context; it does not need a paragraph claiming to be a landmark.

Only describe supported use. A flag named orchard marks a proposed orchard; it is not proof of fruit trees. A tank is not swimming water. A desk is not a seat. Bind a podium part to its actual seating fixture and slot, and distinguish it from audience seating. A description cannot grant an action. Do not repeat that implementation rule in product prose.

Use aliases that an owner might naturally say, without making every place match every query. Use semantic parents for belonging; physical parents stay untouched. Prefer specific district and landmark descriptions over labeling every prop. Mark minor scenery salience0. Salience3 is reserved for the world's defining destinations.

Parts bind existing entities/room/seat slots. Only use a local-point anchor when its explicit level, transform, stand region and actual route have been independently checked. Descriptive parts omit the anchor. Keep names and purpose separate from stable references.

Write each important description so it stands alone in a search result: identify the place, its supported purpose, and the relation or constraint that helps distinguish it. Name the related landmark rather than saying “over there” or relying on the preceding entry. Preserve uncertainty: a proposed orchard is a marker until trees exist; a closed entrance remains closed. Do not turn plausible use into a promised affordance.

For example: “Chiba Auditorium is the campus lecture hall. The presenter’s desk faces the audience bleachers; its chair is the presentation seat.” Bind the chair and bleachers separately to their actual entities/slots. This is more useful than “A room containing a desk, chair and benches.” Add an entrance direction only when it is supported by the current world. A concise identity is enough for an incidental cup or paving tile.

Check the prose against the intended high-level request while writing it: could a stranger distinguish the swimming area, indoor destination, presentation seat or music venue? This is a writing guideline, not a mandatory retrieval-preview or self-review tool loop for every God operation. Physical completion is still established by capabilities, receipts and body state, never by the same prose that suggested the destination.

`overlay.mjs lint <world>` validates schema, source identity, real bindings and unchanged physical geometry. Its advisory writing diagnostics identify the exact ref/field, a stable code and a suggested repair for repeated purpose, internal build refs and duplicate aliases. They do not prove semantic truth, invent missing intent, rewrite content or require a model call. Review warnings in context; an established public name may intentionally resemble an internal ref.
