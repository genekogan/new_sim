# Writing useful world meaning

Name the place for a visitor who has never seen its build references. Say what people do there, one useful relation, and how its important parts differ. Preserve established proper names. Use compact natural prose, not furniture inventories or programming instructions. A ceramic cup needs at most its identity and context; it does not need a paragraph claiming to be a landmark.

Only describe supported use. A flag named orchard marks a proposed orchard; it is not proof of fruit trees. A tank is not swimming water. A desk is not a seat. Bind a podium part to its actual seating fixture and slot, and distinguish it from audience seating. A description cannot grant an action. Do not repeat that implementation rule in product prose.

Use aliases that an owner might naturally say, without making every place match every query. Use semantic parents for belonging; physical parents stay untouched. Prefer specific district and landmark descriptions over labeling every prop. Mark minor scenery salience0. Salience3 is reserved for the world's defining destinations.

Parts bind existing entities/room/seat slots. Only use a local-point anchor when its explicit level, transform, stand region and actual route have been independently checked. Descriptive parts omit the anchor. Keep names and purpose separate from stable references.

Review the highest-salience places as a stranger: could you choose a swimming area, an indoor destination, a presentation seat and a study/music venue from the prose? Then inspect the actual capability/receipt to verify the choice was physically legitimate. Never use the same prose as the completion oracle.
